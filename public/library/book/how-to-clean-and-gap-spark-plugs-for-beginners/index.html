6. Measuring the Spark Plug Gap: Tools and Techniques

Why the Spark Plug Gap Is the Heartbeat of Ignition Imagine you’ve just finished Inspecting and Cleaning Spark Plugs Thoroughly and you’re ready to reinstall them. You bolt the plug back in, start the engine, and it sputters, then dies. The most common hidden culprit? An incorrect spark plug gap. The spark plug gap is the tiny distance—usually measured in thousandths of an inch (0.001 in) or millimetres (mm)—between the center electrode and the ground electrode. When the ignition system fires, a high‑voltage spark must jump that space. If the gap is too small, the spark is weak and may not fully ignite the air‑fuel mixture. If it’s too large, the ignition coil may not be able to generate enough voltage, leading to misfires, rough idle, and loss of power. In short, the gap determines how efficiently the spark plug delivers the energy needed for the Power stroke to happen. --- Tools for Measuring the Gap 1. Feeler Gauge A feeler gauge is a simple, inexpensive tool made of a series of thin metal blades of known thicknesses. Each blade is labeled with its width, allowing you to slide the appropriate blade between the electrodes and read the measurement directly. Typical features - Material: Stainless steel or phosphor bronze – resists bending and corrosion. - Range: Common sets cover 0.015 in to 0.120 in (0.38 mm to 3.05 mm). - Blade shape: Straight or slightly tapered; tapered blades help reach tight gaps. 2. Wire Gapping Tool (Wire Gauge) A wire gapping tool, often called a wire gauge, consists of a set of pre‑bent wires of precise diameters. You select the wire that matches the desired gap and insert it between the electrodes. Because the wire is round, it can be easier to insert into very small gaps without bending the electrodes. Typical features - Material: Hardened steel or copper‑coated steel. - Diameter range: 0.5 mm to 2.0 mm (0.020 in to 0.079 in). - Design: Some kits include a small “hook” on the end to pull the wire out after measurement. 3. Digital Gap Meter (Optional) For those who prefer a modern read‑out, a digital gap meter uses a calibrated probe and LCD screen to display the measurement. While handy, it is more expensive and not essential for a beginner workshop. --- Preparing the Spark Plug for Accurate Measurement Even the best tool can give a false reading if the plug isn’t ready. Frequent Mistakes When Working With Spark Plugs 1.1 Over‑torquing the Plug - What happens: Turning a plug too tight stretches the ceramic insulator or cracks the threads. The check‑engine light flickers, and the driver reports a slight loss of power during the power stroke. A quick scan of the fault codes points to a misfire on cylinder 3. Before diving into expensive diagnostics, you decide to perform a classic, low‑cost remedy: remove, clean, and re‑gap the spark plugs. This chapter walks you through that exact hands‑on process, step by step, so you can repeat it on any four‑stroke engine you own. As you work, you’ll document each observation, use a self‑assessment checklist, and learn how to get feedback from a mentor or an online community. By the end, you’ll have not only a cleaner, correctly gapped set of plugs but also a personal record of the maintenance you performed. --- 1.
