Choose a Bookkeeping Method That Fits Your Business 1.1 Spreadsheet vs. Entry‑Level Accounting Software | Feature | Spreadsheet (e.g., Google Sheets, Excel) | Entry‑Level Software (e.g., Wave, QuickBooks Self‑Employed) | |---------|--------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------| | Cost | Free (cloud) or one‑time license | Free tier or low‑monthly subscription (often $10‑$30) | | Ease of Setup | Requires you to design tables & formulas | Pre‑built templates and guided setup | | Automation | Manual entry; you can use formulas for totals | Automatic bank feed, receipt scanning, tax calculations | | Scalability | Works for very small volumes; can become cumbersome | Handles growing transaction volume, multi‑channel sales | | Learning Curve | Basic spreadsheet skills | Slightly higher; tutorials provided by vendors | When to start with a spreadsheet - You expect fewer than 50 transactions per month. - You’re comfortable building simple tables and formulas. - Budget constraints prevent a software subscription. When to upgrade to entry‑level software - You have multiple sales channels (online marketplace, brick‑and‑mortar, pop‑ups). - You need automatic bank reconciliation or receipt capture. - You prefer built‑in tax reports and profit‑and‑loss (P&L) statements. Pro tip: Begin with a spreadsheet and switch to software once monthly transactions consistently exceed 30–40. This transition is smoother when you already have a habit of recording every sale and expense. 1.2 Setting Up Your Chosen Tool Spreadsheet Blueprint 1.
