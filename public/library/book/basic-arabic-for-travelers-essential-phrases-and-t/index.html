1. Arabic Sounds and Script Foundations

A Traveler’s First Encounter: Ordering a Coffee in Cairo You step out of the bustling hotel lobby onto a sun‑lit street. A fragrant breeze carries the scent of freshly brewed Arabic coffee. A friendly vendor smiles and asks, “قهوة؟” (qahwa?). You want to reply, but the letters on the sign above the stall look like a secret code: ق ه و ة. How do those shapes translate into the sounds you just heard? The moment you can recognize and pronounce each of those letters, the door to everyday Arabic opens. This chapter equips you with the tools to read, write, and say every basic Arabic sound—exactly the skill set you need to navigate cafés, markets, and transport hubs across the Arab world. --- 1. 2. Greetings, Politeness, and Small Talk

Warm‑up: The First Encounter Imagine you step off the bus in Marrakech, the sun is high, and a shopkeeper greets you with a friendly smile. Your first words can set the tone for the whole visit. Knowing how to say “hello,” “please,” and “thank you” in Arabic will instantly make you appear courteous and approachable. --- Common Greetings Formal vs. informal Arabic distinguishes between formal and informal greetings, much like English “Good morning” vs. “Hey.” In most tourist settings, a neutral greeting works everywhere. | Arabic (script) | Transliteration | When to use | |-----------------|----------------|-------------| | Marhaban | marḥabān | General “hello,” safe for strangers. | | Ahlan | ahlān | Slightly more informal, like “hi.” | | As-salām ‘alaykum | as‑salaam ‘alaykum | Very respectful, literally “peace be upon you.” Often answered with Wa‑‘alaykum as‑salaam. | | Sabāḥ al‑khayr | sabāḥ al‑khair | “Good morning.” | | Masā’ al‑khayr | masā’ al‑khair | “Good evening.” | Tip: The diacritics you learned in Arabic Sounds and Script Foundations (fatha, damma, kasra) tell you the short vowel sounds. For marḥabān the fatha on م gives “ma‑,” the sukun on ر stops the sound, and the fatha on ح yields “‑ḥa‑.” Pronunciation pointers - ح (ḥ) is a guttural consonant; it is pronounced deeper in the throat than the English “h.” - ص (ṣ) in as‑salaam is an emphatic consonant; give it a slightly heavier, more rounded quality. - ʿ (ʿayn) in ‘alaykum is another guttural sound—think of a brief catch in the throat. Practicing these sounds will make your greetings sound natural and respectful. --- Asking “How are you?” and Responding The question | Arabic (script) | Transliteration | Gender | |-----------------|----------------|--------| | Kayfa ḥāluk? | kayfa ḥāluk? | Male | | Kayfa ḥālik? | kayfa ḥālik? | Female | | Kayfa ḥālukum? | kayfa ḥālukum? | Plural (group) | Kayfa means “how,” and ḥāl means “condition” or “state.” The ending ‑k (male) or ‑ki (female) agrees with the listener’s gender, a pattern you’ll encounter often in Arabic. Short answers - Al‑ḥamdu lillāh, ana bikhayr. Al‑ḥamdu lillāh, ana bikhayr. – “Thanks to God, I am fine.” - Lā ba‘d. – “Not bad.” - Ta‘bān/ta‘bāna – “Tired” (male/female). You can add wa‑anta? (“and you?”) after your answer to keep the conversation flowing. 3. Numbers, Dates, and Time

Counting from Zero to One Hundred Imagine you’ve just arrived at a bustling souk. The vendor greets you with a smile and asks, “كم عدد التفاح؟” Kam ‘adad al‑tuffāḥ? “How many apples?” To answer confidently you need the numbers from 0 – 100. Arabic Numerals vs. Words Arabic uses two parallel systems: Arabic‑Indic digits (٠ ١ ٢ ٣ ٤ ٥ ٦ ٧ ٨ ٩) – the symbols you’ll see on price tags, clocks, and receipts. Spoken words – the forms you’ll pronounce in conversation. Both are read right‑to‑left, just like the script you learned in Arabic Sounds and Script Foundations. The ability to ask for directions, understand simple signboards, and communicate with drivers will turn a confusing moment into a smooth experience. The words you choose in these critical minutes can make all the difference. This section equips you with the essential Arabic phrases and cultural cues you need to call for medical aid, reach the police, recover lost items, and understand common safety signs.
