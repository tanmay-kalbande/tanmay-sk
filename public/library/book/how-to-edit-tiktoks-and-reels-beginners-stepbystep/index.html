A Mistake That Cost a Creator 12 000 Views Emma spent an evening polishing a 45‑second Reel in her favorite desktop editor. She chose a “high‑quality” export setting, saved the file, and uploaded it to Instagram. Within minutes the video appeared blurry, the colors looked washed out, and the caption box cut off half of her carefully crafted description. By the time she realized the issue, the post had already collected a handful of likes and comments—far below the 12 000‑view benchmark she’d hit on a previous video. What happened? Emma exported the video at 1080 × 1920 px (the correct vertical resolution) but used a 30 fps frame rate and a low bitrate that compressed the file too aggressively for Instagram’s algorithm. The result was a file that looked fine on her phone’s gallery but was rejected by the platform’s quality checks, causing Instagram to down‑scale it further and truncate the caption. Think of them as a set of translation rules that tell your video how to speak the language of TikTok or Instagram. Getting these rules right ensures your video keeps its visual fidelity, loads quickly, and meets each platform’s technical limits. Resolution (Pixel Dimensions) - TikTok: 1080 × 1920 px (vertical) – the default for a full‑screen, mobile‑first experience. - Instagram Reels: 1080 × 1920 px as well, but the platform also accepts 720 × 1280 px for quicker uploads. Why vertical? Both platforms are built around a mobile‑first design that favors a 9:16 aspect ratio (vertical video orientation). Any deviation forces the platform to add black bars or crop your content, which can throw off the visual flow you crafted in Chapter 5 (Introduction to Video Editing Software).
