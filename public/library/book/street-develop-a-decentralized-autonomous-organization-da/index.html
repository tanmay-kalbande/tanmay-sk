The Bounty Board: Turn Research into a Hunger Games You know what researchers actually respond to? Money with strings attached. Forget grants. Grants are for people who enjoy filling out 80-page forms to get a check that arrives six months late. Your DAO needs a bounty board—a public, real-time leaderboard where problems are posted, solutions are funded, and failure gets you laughed out of the Discord. 💡 Pro Tip: Think of it like Uber for science. No surge pricing, but definitely surge accountability. How it works: - Problem Posting: Anyone (DAO members, token holders, even random Twitter trolls) can post a research problem. The more urgent/valuable the problem, the higher the payout. No bureaucracy. No peer review panels. Just skin in the game. - Submission: Researchers submit solutions (code, data, papers) to the DAO’s GitHub repo. No paywalled PDFs. No “trust me, bro.” Show your work or GTFO. - Validation: The DAO’s “reproducibility squad” (more on this later) attempts to replicate the results. If it checks out? Payout. If not? Clawback. No excuses. - Reputation: Every successful bounty earns the researcher reputation points (non-transferable, tied to their wallet). More points = more voting power, more access to funding, more clout. Fail too many times? You’re out. ⚠️ Common Mistake: Letting researchers define their own bounties. Oh, you want …

The Law Wasn’t Written for You (And That’s a Problem) Here’s the brutal truth: The legal system was designed for corporations, not DAOs. Corporations have boards, CEOs, and offices. DAOs have anon contributors, smart contracts, and Discord servers. To the law, you look like a cult with a shared bank account. And guess what? Cults don’t get tax breaks. 💡 Pro Tip: The law doesn’t care about your “decentralized” branding. If it walks like a duck (has a treasury, has contributors, has a goal), the SEC will call it a security until proven otherwise. If your DAO has: - An investment of money - In a common enterprise - With an expectation of profit - Derived from the efforts of others …congratulations, you’re a security. And securities require registration, disclosures, and a shtload of paperwork. Fail to comply? Enjoy your new home in Federal Prison: The Resort. ⚠️ Common Mistake: Thinking “decentralization” is a legal shield. It’s not. The SEC has already gone after DAOs (see: The DAO in 2017, Ooki DAO in 2022). Decentralization is a technical concept, not a legal one. --- 2. The second your code, formula, or design hits the internet, it’s a free-for-all buffet—and the sharks are already circling. Patents, trademarks, copyrights: these aren’t just legal jargon. They’re weapons. And if you don’t learn how to wield them, you’re the one getting disarmed. 💡 Pro Tip: Open-source ≠ public domain. Public domain means anyone can do anything with your work. Lab equipment, salaries, coffee to fuel 3 AM breakthroughs—none of it’s free. If you want your DAO to fund research without begging for donations, you need a revenue stream. You release it under an open-source license (e.g., GPL for non-commercial use). 3. Corporations want to use it? Great. They pay for a commercial license. 4. The money goes back into the DAO treasury to fund more research. 🎯 Key Insight: Dual licensing isn’t about greed. It’s about sustainability.
