1. The Neurophysiology of Deep Samadhi States

A seasoned meditator sits in stillness, the mind quieted to the point where the sense of self as an observer dissolves entirely. The breath slows, the body feels weightless, and the distinction between the meditator and the act of meditation vanishes. This is turiya—the fourth state of consciousness, where the very substrate of perception is transcended. Yet for those who have tasted its depths, the question lingers: What happens to the brain when consciousness is no longer an observer of experience, but the only experience that remains? The answer lies not in the absence of neural activity, but in its radical reorganization. Advanced samadhi states—those beyond the threshold of turiya—demand a re-examination of conventional neurophysiological models. This chapter dissects the neural signatures of deep samadhi, mapping the interplay of gamma, theta, and delta waves, the shifts in dopaminergic and serotonergic systems, and the long-term neuroplastic transformations that define the most refined meditative states.
