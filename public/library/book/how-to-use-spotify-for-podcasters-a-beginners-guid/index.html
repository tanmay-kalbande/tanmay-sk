3. Submitting Your Podcast Feed

Why Submitting Your Feed Is the First Real Step Toward Audiences Imagine you’ve just finished recording the pilot episode of “Morning Brew with Maya”—the intro music is perfect, the interview runs smoothly, and the file sits on your computer, ready to be shared. All the hard work you put into planning, scripting, and recording will stay silent unless you tell Spotify where to find it. That “where” is your podcast’s RSS feed URL, a web address that acts like a delivery truck for each new episode you publish. Submitting that feed to the Spotify for Podcasters dashboard is the moment you officially claim your show on the platform and unlock the ability to see real‑time Analytics, reach listeners, and eventually monetize. The steps below walk you through locating that URL, submitting it, verifying ownership, and fixing the most common feed‑related roadblocks. By the end of this chapter you’ll have your show live on Spotify and ready to start gathering streams, unique listeners, and demographic data. --- 1. Finding Your Podcast’s RSS Feed URL 1.1 What Is an RSS Feed, and Why Does It Matter? An RSS (Really Simple Syndication) feed is a standardized XML file that lists every episode of your podcast along with essential metadata (title, description, cover art, etc.). Podcast hosting services automatically update this file whenever you upload a new episode, and platforms like Spotify read it to import your content. Key point: The RSS feed is the single source of truth for your show. If the feed is missing or malformed, Spotify cannot display or stream your episodes. 1.2 Where to Look Depending on Your Host Most hosts place the feed URL in a clearly labeled section of their dashboard. 6. Optimizing Metadata for Discovery

Why Metadata Is the Gateway to New Listeners Imagine you’ve just recorded a riveting interview with a rising indie musician. The conversation is full of stories that would make any music lover hit “play” twice. You upload the episode, hit Publish, and then… nothing. No new streams, no chatter on social media, and the episode sits quietly in the vast ocean of podcasts. What happened? In the world of Spotify for Podcasters, metadata—the title, description, categories, tags, and artwork—acts like a lighthouse. When crafted with discovery in mind, it guides listeners straight to your show. When neglected, even the best content can remain hidden. Metadata is the information that Spotify’s search engine and recommendation algorithms use to match your episode with people who are looking for it. By optimizing each element, you increase the chances that your show appears in search results, shows up in “Because you listened to…” suggestions, and catches the eye of curious browsers. The following sections walk you through each piece of metadata, showing how to turn basic information into a discovery engine that works for you. --- Crafting SEO‑Friendly Episode Titles 1. Understand the Role of Keywords Keywords are the words and phrases listeners type into Spotify’s search bar. Think of them as the bridge between a listener’s intent and your content. When a listener searches for “true crime stories,” an episode titled “True Crime: The Unsolved Case of the Vanishing Painter” is more likely to appear than “Episode 23 – New Content.” First‑time tip: Treat the episode title as a mini‑search result. It should answer the listener’s question before they click. 2. Keep It Clear, Concise, and Descriptive - Length: Aim for 60 characters or fewer. Spotify truncates longer titles on mobile, which can cut off essential words. - Structure: Place the most important words at the beginning. Example: “Mindful Parenting: 5 Tips for Calm Bedtimes” is clearer than “5 Tips for Calm Bedtimes – Mindful Parenting.” - Avoid Clickbait: Over‑hyped titles (“You Won’t Believe What Happens Next!”) may attract clicks but can hurt retention and algorithmic trust. 3. Blend Human Appeal with Search Value A good title balances searchability and readability. Follow this simple formula: Primary Keyword + Hook + Unique Angle Example: - Primary Keyword: “Digital Marketing” - Hook: “2024 Trends” - Unique Angle: “Small‑Business Playbook” Result: “Digital Marketing 2024 Trends: A Small‑Business Playbook” 4. 8. Monetization Options on Spotify

Turning Listeners into Revenue: A Real‑World Snapshot Imagine Maya, host of The Curious Historian, has just uploaded her latest episode about the Silk Road. Over the past month her Show has attracted 45,000 unique listeners and an average listen duration of 22 minutes. After checking the Episode‑level analytics in the Spotify for Podcasters dashboard, she sees a steady growth trend and wonders: “Can I start earning from this momentum without disrupting my audience’s experience?” The answer lies in the three monetization pathways Spotify offers podcasters today: programmatic ad insertion, direct sponsorships, and listener‑support features. This chapter walks you through each option, shows how to set them up, and explains how to read the resulting revenue reports so you can make informed decisions—exactly the knowledge you need after mastering episode uploads and analytics in earlier modules. --- 1. How Spotify’s Advertising Ecosystem Works for Podcasters Spotify’s ad platform is built on two complementary models: | Model | How It Works | Who Benefits | |-------|--------------|--------------| | Programmatic (Dynamic) Ads | Automated buying and selling of ad inventory. When a listener streams an episode, Spotify’s server selects a relevant ad in real time and inserts it into the audio stream. | Podcasters earn per impression (CPM) or per click (CPC) without having to negotiate with advertisers. | | Direct Sponsorships / Branded Content | You (or your sales team) negotiate a deal with a brand. The agreed‑upon ad copy is recorded by you (host‑read) or supplied by the sponsor and then inserted into your episodes. | Brands get a personal touch; podcasters receive a flat fee or revenue share. | Key Terms (first mention) Dynamic ad insertion – The technology that allows ads to be swapped in or out of an episode after it has been published, based on listener data, location, or timing. CPM (Cost‑Per‑Mille) – The amount an advertiser pays for one thousand ad impressions. CPC (Cost‑Per‑Click) – The amount an advertiser pays each time a listener clicks a clickable ad (e.g., a “Learn More” button). Branded content – Advertising that is integrated into the podcast’s editorial voice, often delivered as a host‑read spot. Spotify handles the heavy lifting for programmatic ads. Once you enable the feature, the platform automatically matches eligible episodes with advertisers from the Spotify Audience Network (SAN). For direct sponsorships, you retain control over the creative and pricing, but you still use Spotify’s insertion engine to deliver the spot. --- 2. The episode is missing, and the dashboard shows a red warning icon next to the episode title. You’re not alone; many new podcasters hit this wall within their first few weeks. The good news is that the problem almost always has a clear cause and an equally clear fix. Below we walk through the most common roadblocks—RSS feed validation errors, upload or processing hiccups, and audio‑quality glitches—and give you the tools to resolve them quickly. --- 1. Diagnosing RSS Feed Validation Errors Your RSS (Really Simple Syndication) feed is the backbone that tells Spotify where to find each episode file and its associated metadata. 11. Best Practices and Next Steps

Turning a First-Time Podcast Into a Lasting Audience Imagine it’s six weeks after your show’s first episode drops. The notifications are rolling in: your stats show 2,842 streams, but only 415 are unique listeners, and the average listen duration is just 7 minutes. You’re proud of the content, but the numbers feel flat. What’s missing isn’t another episode—it’s a plan for what happens after the episode is live. This chapter shows how to keep momentum alive, turn casual listeners into regulars, and use the free Spotify for Podcasters dashboard as your command center. --- Build a Publishing Schedule That Feels Like Clockwork A predictable release rhythm is the heartbeat of any podcast. It tells listeners when to expect new material and signals to Spotify’s algorithm that your show is active and reliable. Without it, episodes can disappear into the noise. Pick a Frequency You Can Sustain - Weekly: The sweet spot for most beginners. It keeps content fresh without burning you out. - Biweekly: Easier on your production schedule, but can feel slow if your topic thrives on timeliness. - Monthly: Manageable for deep dives, but harder to build momentum. Use only if your format demands long production cycles. Pro tip: Start with biweekly or monthly if weekly feels overwhelming. It’s better to launch strong and scale than to fade out after four episodes. Set a “Drop Day” and Stick to It Choose a day that fits your life and audience’s habits. Tuesday and Thursday mornings often see higher initial engagement, but test what works for your listeners. Batch Record and Schedule Ahead Record 2–3 episodes in one sitting, then upload them to your feed with scheduled release dates. This buffer protects you from last-minute rushes and keeps your feed from going dark. Scenario: You’re a solo podcaster who works full-time. Every second Sunday, you block off two hours to record and edit. You schedule the next three episodes to release on the first and third Thursdays of the month. This routine ensures you never miss a deadline. --- Turn Listeners Into Active Participants The most successful podcasts don’t just broadcast—they converse. Engagement isn’t a one-time event; it’s a loop of listening, responding, and refining. Make Reviews and Ratings Part of Your Routine Every time you publish an episode: 1. Ask for a review in your outro. Keep it simple: “If you enjoyed this episode, please leave a review on Spotify. It helps more people discover the show.” 2. Respond to reviews when possible. A quick “Thank you!” under a positive review builds connection. Even addressing criticism politely can turn a detractor into an advocate. 3. Share highlights in your next episode.
