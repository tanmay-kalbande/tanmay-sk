1. 1. Account Setup & Interface Overview

Getting Started – From a Blank Screen to Your First Monday.com Account Imagine you’ve just been handed the reins of a small marketing team that works across three time zones. Your manager wants a single place to track campaign ideas, assign tasks, and see progress at a glance—but you’ve never used Monday.com before. The first question that pops up isn’t “How do I build a board?”; it’s “How do I even get inside the platform?” This chapter walks you through that exact moment: creating a Monday.com account, picking a plan that matches a beginner’s needs, and learning the layout of the interface so you can feel comfortable navigating the tool from day one. --- 1. Creating a Monday.com Account 1.1 The Sign‑Up Flow 1. Visit the homepage – Go to monday.com. The landing page greets you with a brief value proposition and a prominent “Start for free” button. 2. Enter your email – Type the email address you’ll use for work (or a personal email if you’re just testing). Monday.com will automatically detect whether the domain belongs to a known company and may suggest a pre‑filled workspace name. 3. Choose a password – Create a strong password (at least 8 characters, a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols). Monday.com offers a strength meter to guide you. 4. Confirm your email – Check your inbox for a verification message and click the link inside. This step protects your account from unauthorized access. 5. Answer a quick onboarding questionnaire – You’ll be asked about the size of your team, the type of work you do, and whether you’re looking for a personal or team solution. The answers help Monday.com suggest the most relevant features, but you can skip or edit them later. Tip: If you already have a Google or Microsoft account, you can click the corresponding “Sign up with Google” or “Sign up with Microsoft” button. This creates a Monday.com account linked to your existing credentials, saving you a password to remember. 1.2 Verifying Your Identity (Optional but Recommended) For added security, especially if you’ll be handling sensitive data, enable Two‑Factor Authentication (2FA) after logging in: - Navigate to Profile → Settings → Security. - Choose either an authentication app (e.g., Authy, Google Authenticator) or SMS codes. - Follow the on‑screen prompts to scan a QR code or receive a text message. Once 2FA is active, each login will require the second factor, dramatically reducing the risk of unauthorized entry. --- 2. 2. 2. Understanding Workspaces and Boards

The Building Blocks of Monday.com: Workspaces, Boards, and Groups Imagine you’ve just been hired to coordinate the launch of a new product line. Your team includes designers, engineers, marketers, and sales reps—each with their own tasks, deadlines, and files. How do you keep everyone’s work visible, separate, yet still part of the same overall project? In Monday.com, the answer lies in the three‑tier hierarchy of Workspaces → Boards → Groups. Think of it as a set of nested containers, each serving a distinct purpose: | Level | What it Holds | Typical Use | Who Can See It | |-------|---------------|-------------|----------------| | Workspace | One or more Boards | A logical collection of related projects (e.g., “Product Launch 2024”) | All members you invite to the workspace | | Board | Items (rows) organized into Columns | A single project, workflow, or process (e.g., “Design Sprint – Phase 1”) | Everyone in the workspace, unless the board is set to private | | Group | A subset of items within a board | Stages, categories, or teams (e.g., “To‑Do”, “In Review”, “Completed”) | Same audience as the board itself | Understanding how these pieces fit together is the first step toward turning Monday.com into a digital command center for your work. --- 1. Workspaces: Your Top‑Level Hub 1.1 What Is a Workspace? A workspace is the highest‑level container in Monday.com. It groups together boards that belong to the same department, client, product line, or any other logical collection you choose. Because workspaces sit directly under your account, they inherit the same subscription plan (Free, Basic, Standard, etc.) you selected during Account Setup & Interface Overview. 1.2 Why Use Multiple Workspaces? - Separation of concerns – Keep the marketing team’s boards distinct from engineering’s, avoiding clutter. - Permission control – Invite only the relevant people to each workspace, limiting access to sensitive data. - Reporting clarity – Generate workspace‑level analytics to see how each business unit is performing. 1.3 Creating Your First Workspace 1. Navigate to the left‑hand sidebar (the navigation panel you saw after signing in). 2. Click “+ New Workspace” at the bottom of the list. 3. Name your workspace – e.g., “Product Launch 2024”. 4. (Optional) Add a short description to remind members of its purpose. 5. Click “Create”. Tip: If you’re on the Free plan, you can create up to three workspaces. Upgrade to Basic or higher for unlimited workspaces. 1.4 Inviting Team Members to a Workspace 1. Open the workspace you just created. 2. 3. 3. Building Your First Board

Starting with a Real‑World Need Imagine you’ve just been tasked with launching a new product line for a small company. The marketing lead wants a single place where the team can track ideas, assign responsibilities, set deadlines, and see the overall progress at a glance. You’ve heard that Monday.com can serve as that hub, but you’ve never built a board before. This chapter walks you through exactly that first step: turning a blank canvas—or a ready‑made template—into a functional board that matches your project’s needs. Pro tip: If you’re still logged into the account you created in Chapter 1 – Account Setup & Interface Overview, you’ll see the familiar Monday.com homepage with your Workspace already displayed. All the actions below assume you’re starting from that view. --- Choosing a Starting Point: Template vs. Blank Board When a Template Makes Sense Templates are pre‑configured boards that come with a set of columns, default views, and sample items. They save you time when the underlying structure matches a common workflow—project management, sales pipeline, event planning, etc. Typical scenarios for using a template - You need a proven structure (e.g., a Gantt‑style timeline for a construction project). - Your team prefers a visual that’s already familiar (e.g., Kanban board for agile teams). - You want to explore Monday.com’s capabilities before customizing everything yourself. Accessing the Template Library 1. From the homepage, locate the + New button in the upper‑right corner. 2. Click + New → Board. 3. In the dialog that appears, choose “Use a template” instead of “Start from scratch.” 4. Browse the categories (Project Management, CRM, Marketing, etc.) or type a keyword in the search bar. Example: For the product launch, the “Project Management” template provides columns like Status, Timeline, Owner, and Priority—a solid foundation for tracking tasks. Selecting and Instantiating a Template 1. Click the thumbnail of the desired template. 2. A preview screen shows the board’s layout and a short description. 3. Hit “Use this template.” 4. You’ll be prompted to name your new board and choose the Workspace where it will reside (the workspace you explored in Chapter 2 – Understanding Workspaces and Boards). 5. Press Create Board. Monday.com instantly generates a copy of the template, preserving the column types and any sample items the template includes. You can now start editing it to fit your specific project. The next step is to start adding items – the individual tasks, ideas, or pieces of work that will fill the board. 1. The Quick‑Add Toolbar At the top of every board sits the Quick‑Add toolbar (the plus ✚ icon next to the board name). Clicking it opens a tiny form: 1. Enter a name – This becomes the item’s title (e.g., “Write press release”). 2. Choose a group – By default the item lands in the first group (often “Group 1”). You can switch groups later. 3. Press Enter – The item appears as a new row on the board. Tip: Hover over the plus ✚ icon; a tooltip reminds you that you can also press Enter while a column is selected to add an item instantly. 2. Adding Multiple Items at Once If you have a list of tasks prepared in a spreadsheet, you can paste them directly: Copy the column of task names. Click the + Add item button at the bottom of the board. Paste – Monday.com will create a separate item for each line. This “bulk‑add” method is a lifesaver when you’re migrating from an old to‑do list. 3. Editing Item Details Once an item exists, click its name to open the Item Details pane on the right. Here you can: Rename the item. Add a description – useful for context, links, or notes. Attach files – drag‑and‑drop or click the paper‑clip icon. Add comments – a lightweight way to start a conversation (covered later in the Collaboration chapter). All changes are saved automatically; there’s no “Save” button to hunt for. 5. 5. Views, Filters, and Sorting

Seeing Your Work From Every Angle Imagine you’re managing a product launch timeline. In the Table view you see every task, its owner, due date, and status. When you switch to Kanban, the same tasks line up in columns labeled “Ideas,” “In Progress,” and “Done,” giving you a quick visual of work‑in‑progress. Flip to Timeline and the dates stretch across a Gantt‑style bar, revealing overlaps and bottlenecks. Finally, the Calendar view drops each task onto its actual due date, letting you spot busy weeks at a glance. All of these perspectives live on the same board—you don’t need to recreate anything. The power of Monday.com comes from being able to toggle between views, filter for exactly what you need, and sort items so the most relevant information bubbles to the top. Let’s walk through each step, using the product‑launch board as our running example. --- Switching Between Views Monday.com ships with several default views, each designed to answer a different question: | View | When to Use It | What You See | |------|----------------|--------------| | Table | Detailed data entry, bulk edits | Rows (items) and columns (attributes) | | Kanban | Workflow stages, visual progress | Cards stacked in columns representing statuses | | Timeline | Project planning, dependencies | Bars spanning dates, optional dependencies | | Calendar | Scheduling, deadline awareness | Items plotted on a monthly/weekly calendar | Adding a New View 1. Locate the View Bar – It sits just above the board’s columns, right of the board name. 2. Click the “+ Add view” button (a plus sign). 3. Choose the view type you want (Table, Kanban, Timeline, Calendar, or any of the premium views you may have later). 4. Give the view a descriptive name (e.g., “Launch Kanban”) and hit Create. Your new view appears as a tab next to the existing ones. Click any tab to switch instantly—no page reload required. Quick‑Switch Tips Keyboard shortcut: Press V to open the view selector, then arrow keys to pick a view. Default view: The first view you create becomes the board’s default when teammates open the board. You can change the default later by clicking the three‑dot menu on the view tab and selecting Set as default. Mobile app: Swipe left/right on the view bar to cycle through views on a phone or tablet. Customizing a View Each view has its own settings panel (gear icon). For example, in Timeline you can: Toggle Show weekends on/off. Enable Dependencies to draw arrows between tasks. 6. 6. Introduction to Automations

Why Automate? A Real‑World Snapshot Imagine you’re managing a product‑launch board in Monday.com. Every time a task moves from “In Progress” to “Completed,” you need to email the project lead, update a status column, and add the item to a “Finished Tasks” view. Doing this manually for dozens of items each day wastes time and invites errors. Automation solves this problem by letting Monday.com perform those repetitive actions for you—instantly and consistently. Think of it as a “if‑this‑then‑that” engine built into every board, turning simple triggers (like a status change) into automatic outcomes (like sending a notification). With the right automation, you free up mental bandwidth to focus on strategic work instead of ticking off check‑list items. --- What Is an Automation? In Monday.com, an automation is a rule that connects a trigger (the “if” part) to an action (the “then” part). When the trigger condition occurs on a board, Monday.com automatically executes the defined action. - Trigger – The event that starts the automation. Common triggers include: - A column value changes (e.g., status, date, text) - An item is created or moved - A deadline approaches or passes - Action – What Monday.com does once the trigger fires. Typical actions include: - Sending a notification or email - Updating a column value - Creating a new item or duplicate - Assigning a user or moving an item to another board Because automations live inside the board you’re already working with, they respect the same permissions, views, and filters you set up in earlier chapters (see Chapters 2‑5). No separate tool or coding language is required. --- When to Use Automations | Situation | Benefit of an Automation | |-----------|--------------------------| | Status changes (e.g., “Ready for Review”) | Instantly alert the right person, avoid missed hand‑offs | | Upcoming deadlines | Proactively remind owners, reduce overdue tasks | | Recurring tasks (e.g., weekly reports) | Auto‑create new items, keep the rhythm without manual entry | | Data consistency (e.g., keep a “Total Hours” column synced) | Reduce human error, maintain up‑to‑date numbers | | Cross‑board updates (e.g., moving a lead from “Prospects” to “Clients”) | Keep multiple boards aligned without duplicate work | If you find yourself performing the same manual steps repeatedly, an automation is likely the answer. --- Setting Up Your First Automation: Status‑Change Notification Let’s walk through a concrete example that builds on the board you created in Chapter 3 (“Building Your First Board”). 8. 8. Collaboration & Communication Tools

Posting Updates and @‑Mentioning Teammates What an “Update” Is In Monday.com an Update is a short, time‑stamped message that lives directly on an item (the row you created in Building Your First Board). Think of it as a mini‑conversation thread attached to a specific task, bug, or feature. Updates keep all the chatter about that item in one place, so you don’t have to chase emails or jump between chats. Why it matters: When a designer uploads a new mock‑up, a developer can comment “Looks good – I’ll start coding tomorrow” right on the same item. Everyone sees the context instantly. Where to Find the Update Box 1. Open the board that contains the item you want to discuss. 2. Click the item’s cell (any column) to open the item’s detail pane on the right side of the screen. 3. At the bottom of this pane you’ll see a gray bar that says “Write an update…” – that’s the entry field. If you’ve already expanded the pane in a previous session, the field will be visible the moment the pane opens. Writing an Effective Update | Step | Action | Tip | |------|--------|-----| | 1 | Start with a clear subject – Monday.com automatically captures the first line as a preview in the board view. | Example: “🚀 Launch assets ready for review” | | 2 | State the purpose – “I’ve uploaded the final branding pack.” | Keep it to one‑two sentences. | | 3 | Add any required next steps – “Please approve by EOD Friday.” | Use bold (bold) for deadlines or important actions. | | 4 | End with a call‑to‑action – “@JaneDoe, can you take a look?” | This leads naturally into the @‑mention step. | Using @‑Mentions An @‑mention is a way to notify a specific teammate (or a whole team) that their attention is needed. When you type @ followed by a name, Monday.com suggests matching users; click the right one and the name turns blue. - @‑mentioning a person – Sends them a notification (email, in‑app, or mobile push, depending on their personal settings). - @‑mentioning a team – Use @TeamName to notify every member of that team. Great for cross‑functional work (e.g., “@MarketingTeam, please add the social copy”). Best practice: Mention only the people who truly need to act. Over‑mentioning can lead to notification fatigue. 9. 9. Best Practices & Ongoing Management

Naming Conventions: Your Board’s First Line of Defense Imagine opening a shared workspace three months from now, staring at a list of boards with names like "Project X", "Untitled Board 12", and "Do Not Touch". The panic sets in: Which one is for the client report due Friday? Which board tracks our marketing campaign? Without clear naming, Monday.com becomes a digital junk drawer—useful but unnavigable. Naming conventions are the unsung heroes of organization. They’re the difference between a board that grows organically (and chaotically) and one that remains a tool, not a burden. Think of them as the address system for your work: without consistent street names, even the most detailed map is useless. Why Naming Matters (Beyond Just Avoiding "Untitled Board 17") 1. Clarity for Teams: A name like "Q3 Marketing Campaign - Social Media" is instantly recognizable. Compare that to "Untitled Board (1)"—which requires opening the board to understand its purpose. 2. Search Efficiency: Monday.com’s search function (the magnifying glass in the top-right corner) relies on names. A board called "Client Onboarding: Acme Corp" will surface faster than one titled "Jan's Stuff". 3. Scalability: As your usage grows, conventions prevent naming collisions. For example, if every team prefixes their boards with their department (e.g., "HR - New Hire Onboarding", "Finance - Quarterly Reports"), duplicates become obvious. 4. Professionalism: For client-facing boards, names like "Website Redesign - Client A" reflect organization. Names like "Stuff to Do" undermine credibility. Building Your Naming System: Start Simple, Then Refine You don’t need a rigid, corporate-style naming taxonomy on day one. Begin with a lightweight system and adjust as you scale. Here’s a step-by-step approach: 1. Boards: The Big Picture Start with the purpose of the board. Ask: - What is the primary function of this board? (e.g., tracking projects, managing tasks, storing resources) - Who is the primary audience? (e.g., your team, a client, cross-functional partners) - What timeframe does it cover? (e.g., a sprint, a quarter, a one-off event) Example formats: - Project-based: [Project Name] - [Purpose] → "Website Redesign - Development Tracker" - Departmental: [Department] - [Purpose] → "Marketing - Campaign Q3 2024" - Time-bound: [Timeframe] - [Purpose] → "2024-10 - Budget Review" - Client-specific: [Client Name] - [Purpose] → "Acme Corp - Contract Renewal" Pro tip: Avoid vague terms like "Dashboard" or "Tracking." If a board is for tracking, the name should reflect what is being tracked. 2. Groups: Breaking Boards into Logical Chunks Groups are sub-sections within a board (e.g., "To Do," "In Progress," "Done" in a task board).
