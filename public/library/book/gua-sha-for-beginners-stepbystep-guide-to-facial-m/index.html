By the end, you’ll be speaking the language of Gua Sha fluently—not just moving the tool, but guiding it with intention. --- Scraping vs. Lifting: The Core Distinction Gua Sha is often described as “scraping,” but that word can be misleading. The goal isn’t to scrape the skin like a grater through cheese. Instead, think of controlled lifting with a smooth, gliding motion. The tool doesn’t drag across the skin—it moves with the skin, gently separating and smoothing the underlying layers. - Scraping (incorrect): Pressing too hard, dragging the tool in short, choppy motions, creating redness or irritation. - Lifting (correct): Gliding the tool in long, fluid strokes, using light to moderate pressure to encourage movement rather than force. The key difference lies in direction and pressure. Scraping pushes against tension; lifting guides toward release. When done properly, lifting motions support stimulating microcirculation and encouraging lymphatic drainage, helping to reduce facial puffiness and improve skin radiance. 💡 Think of your Gua Sha tool as a gentle wave—it doesn’t break the surface, it rides it. --- The Three Primary Gua Sha Strokes Every Gua Sha routine is built from three foundational strokes. What happens at the cellular level? 1. Micro‑circulation boost – The gentle pressure of the tool squeezes capillaries, encouraging fresh blood to flow into the dermal layers. 2.
