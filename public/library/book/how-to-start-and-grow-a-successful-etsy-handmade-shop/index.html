1. Understanding Handmade Products and the Etsy Marketplace

What Exactly Counts as “Handmade” on Etsy? Imagine you just finished carving a set of wooden spoons. They’re smooth to the touch, each handle has a unique grain pattern, and you can still smell the beeswax you used to finish them. You snap a photo, write a quick listing, and hit “publish.” Three days later an order lands in your inbox. That’s the dream, right? Now imagine the same scenario, but instead of carving the spoons yourself, you bought plain, mass-produced blanks from a big-box store, sanded them lightly, and added a sticker that says “hand-finished.” Is that still handmade? What if you 3-D printed the spoons but assembled the packaging by hand—does that count? Etsy’s rules exist to keep the marketplace authentic for both buyers and sellers, so let’s clear up the confusion before you invest time or money. Etsy’s Three Core Rules for Handmade Etsy’s Handmade Policy is built around three pillars. Stick to them and you’re good; bend them and you risk account suspension. 1. You must make the item yourself - You (or your immediate team) must personally create the physical item from start to finish. - “You” can mean an individual, a family, or a small team of makers who work side-by-side in the same physical space. 2. You can use tools and equipment, but not mass manufacturing - Power tools, kilns, sewing machines, 3-D printers, looms—all fine as long as the machine isn’t doing the creative heavy lifting for you. - Mass manufacturing is the red line. If the process could be duplicated by an assembly line—even a small one—Etsy considers it outside the spirit of handmade. 3. You can use “outside” materials as long as they’re transformed - Pre-made components are allowed only if you significantly alter them. For example: - Buying blank ceramic tiles and hand-painting them (OK). - Buying plain cotton fabric and sewing it into a tote bag (OK). - Buying pre-assembled jewelry findings and stringing beads onto them (OK). - What’s not allowed: simply assembling pre-made parts without meaningful transformation (e.g., putting a branded charm on a mass-produced bracelet). The “Made by You or Your Small Team” Test Etsy doesn’t cap the size of your “team,” but it does cap the distance between you and the production. If you outsource cutting, sewing, or assembly to a factory or remote workshop that you don’t personally oversee, Etsy considers it outsourcing rather than handmade. Scenario check: - Allowed: You run a candle shop with your spouse; you pour wax while they design labels. - Not allowed: You design the candle label but hire a third-party workshop to pour and package 100 units. Let’s break it down. Business Licenses: Do You Need One? A business license is an official permit from your city, county, or state that allows you to operate a business. But here’s the catch: whether you need one depends on where you live and what you sell. - No universal rule: Some cities require all businesses to have a license, while others only require one if you’re operating under a name other than your legal name. - Home-based businesses: Many local governments exempt very small businesses from licensing if you’re operating from home and earning under a certain threshold (often $1,000–$5,000 per year). - Check your local laws: The best place to start is your city or county website. Look for a “business license” or “doing business as” (DBA) section. If you’re unsure, call your local city hall or small business development center. 💡 Tip: Even if you don’t need a license right away, it’s smart to register your business name (if you’re not using your legal name) and keep records in case you expand later.
