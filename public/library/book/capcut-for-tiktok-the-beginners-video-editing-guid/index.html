1. Getting Started with CapCut

Imagine this: You’re at a coffee shop with a friend, and you capture a perfect, five-second clip of them accidentally spilling their latte while trying to take a photo of it. It’s hilarious. It has viral written all over it. You immediately open TikTok, select the video, and hit post. But as you watch it later, you realize the first three seconds are just you fumbling with your phone, and the last two seconds feature your friend’s awkward apology. The funny part is buried in the middle. If only you could just chop off the bad parts. Enter CapCut. Created by ByteDance—the same company behind TikTok—CapCut is a free, wildly powerful mobile video editor designed specifically for short-form content. It bridges the gap between raw, clumsy footage and the polished, fast-paced videos that dominate social media today. You don't need a thousand-dollar computer or a film school degree to use it. All you need is your phone. Downloading and Installing CapCut Before you can transform your raw clips into scroll-stopping content, you need to get the app onto your device. CapCut is available for both iOS and Android, and the process takes only a couple of minutes. Step-by-Step Installation 1. Open your app store: If you are on an iPhone or iPad, open the App Store. If you are on an Android device, open the Google Play Store. 2. Search for the app: Tap the search bar and type "CapCut." Look for the app with a black icon featuring a stylized white image that looks like a combination of scissors and the letter "C." The developer should be listed as "Bytedance." 3. Download: Tap the "Get" button (iOS) or the "Install" button (Android). The app is completely free, though it does offer optional premium subscriptions for advanced features. For TikTok editing, the free version is more than enough. 4. Open the app: Once installed, tap "Open" to launch CapCut for the first time. Setting Up Your Workspace When you open CapCut for the first time, the app will ask for permission to access your photos and media. Tap "Allow." If you deny this permission, you will not be able to import your videos into the app later. You might also be prompted to agree to terms of service and privacy policies. Read through them if you wish, then tap "Agree and Continue." Finally, CapCut may ask if you want to sign in using your TikTok, Apple, Google, or email account. Signing in is entirely optional, but it does allow you to back up your projects to the cloud so you can switch between devices without losing your work. 2. Basic Cutting and Trimming

You press record, nail the perfect intro, and then spend the next ten seconds fumbling with your phone to get your lighting right. Or maybe you capture a brilliant moment, but the camera keeps rolling for thirty seconds afterward while you walk away to set your phone down. Raw video footage is rarely ready for TikTok straight out of the camera. The magic of a captivating TikTok video isn't just in what you film; it’s in what you keep and what you throw away. To create the fast-paced, engaging videos that perform well on TikTok, you need to learn how to sculpt your raw footage. In the previous chapter, you learned how to open CapCut, start a New Project, and bring your video files into the app using the importing process. Now, it is time to take those raw Clips on your Timeline and turn them into a story. The Art of the Cut: Splitting Clips When you film a video, it usually comes into CapCut as one long, continuous block. If you want to remove a mistake in the middle of that video, you can't just delete the whole thing. You need to isolate the bad part from the good parts. In video editing, we do this by Splitting. Splitting takes one long clip and cuts it into two separate, smaller clips. Once a clip is split into pieces, you can delete the pieces you don't want, or rearrange them. How to Split a Clip To split a clip, you will use the Playhead—the vertical white line with a handle on top that we introduced in Chapter 1. The Playhead acts as your editing blade. Wherever the Playhead sits on the Timeline, that is exactly where the cut will happen. 1. Drag the Playhead across the Timeline until it is positioned exactly at the moment you want to cut. You can use the Preview Screen to watch your video and stop at the exact frame. 2. Tap on the video clip in the Timeline to make sure it is selected (it will usually be highlighted with a white border). 3. Look at the Main Toolbar at the bottom of the screen. 4. Tap the Split button (it looks like a small rectangle with a vertical dashed line through the middle). Instantly, your single clip will become two clips. You will see a small, sharp vertical line where the cut was made. Removing Unwanted Footage Splitting a clip doesn't delete anything; it just creates a seam. To actually remove the unwanted footage, you need to split it on both sides, effectively isolating the bad section. 3. Audio and Sound Design

Think about the last time you scrolled past a video on TikTok where the music was slightly too quiet, or the creator’s voice was completely drowned out by a loud beat. You probably kept scrolling. On a platform driven by sound, bad audio can ruin a perfectly good video, while great audio can make a simple clip go viral. In Basic Cutting and Trimming, you learned how to slice your video clips and arrange them on the Timeline to create a visual flow. Now, it is time to breathe life into that structure by giving your video a voice. Whether you are adding a trending song, pulling a funny quote from another video, or narrating your own content, CapCut gives you all the tools you need to build a rich soundscape. The Basics of Sound in CapCut Before we start adding tracks, it helps to understand how CapCut handles audio. In Getting Started with CapCut, we discussed how your video clips sit on the Timeline as Layers. Audio works the exact same way. When you add music, extract a sound, or record a voiceover, CapCut places that audio on its own separate layer beneath your main video clip. Because audio lives on its own layer, you can move it, trim it, or delete it without messing up your video clips. There are three primary ways to add audio to your project: 1. Adding music from CapCut’s built-in library or your device’s storage. 2. Extracting audio from an existing video file saved on your phone. 3. Recording a voiceover directly within the app using your phone’s microphone. Let’s walk through how to do each one. Adding Background Music Background music sets the mood for your TikTok. It might be a trending pop song, a lo-fi beat for a tutorial, or a dramatic orchestral swell for a comedic reveal. CapCut makes it incredibly easy to browse and add music. Accessing the Audio Menu To add music to your project: 1. Open your project in CapCut. 2. Look at the Main Toolbar located beneath the Timeline. 3. Tap the Audio button. This opens a new menu with several options. The two you will use most often are Sounds (CapCut’s built-in music library) and From device (audio files saved directly on your phone). 4. Text, Captions, and Stickers

Imagine you are scrolling through TikTok on a crowded train. You forgot your headphones, and even if you had them, the person next to you is already giving you side-eye for playing loud audio in public. You keep scrolling, but you stop on one video. Why? Because it has large, clear captions right in the middle of the screen. You can read exactly what the creator is saying without needing to hear a single sound. A massive portion of TikTok viewing happens with the sound off. If your videos rely entirely on audio to deliver their message, you are losing viewers. Text, captions, and stickers bridge that gap. They grab attention, provide context, and make your content accessible to everyone. In Basic Cutting and Trimming, you learned how to slice your raw footage into a cohesive story. In Audio and Sound Design, you layered sounds and music beneath that story. Now, it is time to add the visual information layer. This chapter covers how to add manual text boxes, generate hands-free automatic captions, apply animated stickers, and even turn your written text into spoken voiceovers. Adding and Styling Manual Text Boxes Sometimes you do not need captions for your voice; you just need a title, a call-to-action, or a quick bit of context. This is where manual text boxes come in. A Text Box is a customizable layer of written words that sits on top of your video. Just like audio tracks or video clips, text exists on its own Layer in your Timeline. Creating a Text Box 1. Open your project in CapCut and move your Playhead to the exact moment you want the text to appear. 2. Tap the Text button located on the Main Toolbar at the bottom of the screen. 3. Tap Add text. 4. A cursor will appear on the Preview Screen, and a keyboard will pop up. Type your desired message (e.g., "Wait for it..." or "Follow for Part 2!"). 5. Tap Done on the keyboard. Once you hit Done, you will notice a new text layer appears in your Timeline. It looks slightly different from a video clip—usually represented by a colored bar with a "T" icon. Positioning and Timing By default, your new text will sit in the center of the screen and last for a few seconds. You can easily change both of these attributes. Moving the text: Tap and drag the text box directly on the Preview Screen. Drag it to the top, bottom, or sides. 5. Transitions and Visual Effects

Why Transitions and Effects Matter on TikTok Imagine a standard TikTok video: a creator is showing you how they organize their morning routine. They wake up, they make coffee, they journal. If they just leave the camera running, you’d have to watch thirty seconds of them staring blankly at a wall waiting for their coffee to brew. Because you already learned how to cut out those boring parts in Basic Cutting and Trimming, your video is now fast-paced. But when you splice two completely different clips together, the sudden change of scene can feel jarring to the viewer. This is where transitions step in. A transition is a bridge between two clips that smooths out the jump or adds a stylistic flourish. Alongside transitions, we have visual effects and filters. Think of effects and filters as the makeup of video editing. A filter might give your video a warm, vintage 1970s look, while a visual effect might add a dreamy glow around your head or blur out the background. When used correctly, these three tools elevate a standard video into a highly engaging TikTok. But when used incorrectly, they can turn a good video into a dizzying mess. Let’s look at how to master them in CapCut. The Anatomy of a Transition Before we start tapping buttons, it helps to understand what a transition actually does to your Timeline. In CapCut, a transition is not a new clip. Instead, it is an effect that overlaps two existing clips. When you apply a transition, CapCut takes the end of your first clip and the beginning of your second clip and merges them together for a set amount of time. For example, if you have a 10-second clip and a 10-second clip, and you add a 1-second transition between them, your total video time will drop to 19 seconds. One second of the clips is now overlapping. Finding and Applying Transitions To add a transition, look at the Timeline where two clips meet. You will see a small white box with a vertical line down the middle. This is the Transition Button. 1. Tap the Transition Button between two clips on your Timeline. 2. The Transition menu will pop up from the bottom of the screen. 3. Here, you will see categories like Basic, Overlay, Speed Blur, and Glitch. 4. Scroll horizontally to preview the options. When you tap a transition (like "Mix" or "Pull In"), the Preview Screen will automatically play a quick loop of what it looks like. 5. 6. Speed Control and Keyframes

Why Speed and Movement Matter on TikTok Imagine watching a cooking video where the chef chops vegetables in real-time. You sit through thirty seconds of slow, methodical slicing before anything exciting happens. Chances are, you swipe away. Now imagine that same video, but the chopping is sped up four times, set to a catchy beat, and the camera slowly zooms in on the knife as it hits the cutting board. That sudden burst of speed and subtle movement grabs your eye and holds your attention. On TikTok, pacing is everything. If a video drags, viewers leave. By manipulating the speed of your clips, you can compress boring tasks into seconds or stretch a fleeting moment into a dramatic slow-motion sweep. But speed alone isn't enough. A completely static video can still feel lifeless. To truly mimic the dynamic feel of professional content, you need movement. This is where keyframes come in. Keyframes allow you to animate your clips—making them zoom, pan, and drift across the screen. Together, speed control and keyframes are the secret ingredients to creating the fast-paced, visually engaging style that dominates the TikTok algorithm. The Basics of Speed Control When you record a video on your phone, it plays back at normal speed—usually 1.0x. Every second of recorded video takes exactly one second to play. Speed control is the process of deliberately changing this playback rate. When you speed a clip up, you compress time. A ten-second recording might play back in just three seconds. When you slow a clip down, you expand time. A two-second recording might stretch out to take six seconds to play. In CapCut, adjusting the speed of a clip is incredibly straightforward. Because you learned how to navigate the Timeline and select clips in earlier chapters, you already know how to target the specific piece of media you want to manipulate. Uniform Speed: Speeding Up and Slowing Down The most common way to adjust speed is using the Uniform Speed tool. "Uniform" simply means the speed stays exactly the same from the first frame of the clip to the last. Here is how to apply it: 1. Open your project in CapCut. 2. 7. Overlays and Background Removal

Imagine you are watching a popular TikTok where a creator is reacting to a funny viral video. On the screen, you see the original viral video playing full-screen, but in the corner, the creator’s own facecam video is playing simultaneously. Both videos play at the exact same time, perfectly synced. How does that work? You might think the creator exported one video, filmed themselves watching it, and uploaded that second recording. While possible, that method destroys video quality and makes syncing audio a nightmare. The secret is layering. Professional editors call it compositing, but in CapCut, it’s all about using overlays. By stacking multiple video layers on top of each other, you can create reactions, show gameplay footage while talking over it, transport yourself to a tropical beach using a green screen, or blend two videos together for a dreamy, artistic effect. In this chapter, we will take the foundational cutting and trimming skills you learned earlier and elevate them by adding a third dimension to your Timeline: depth. Understanding Overlays and the Picture-in-Picture Tool When you start a New Project and import your first video, that clip sits on the base level of your Timeline. Think of this base level as the background canvas of your video. Everything you add on top of it is an overlay. An overlay is simply a second video, photo, or graphic that plays simultaneously on top of your main video. To add an overlay in CapCut, you use the Picture-in-Picture (PiP) feature. Adding a Picture-in-Picture Track When you use PiP, CapCut creates a new layer on your Timeline, directly above your main video track. Here is how to do it: 1. Open your main video project in CapCut. 2. Ensure your main video clip is selected on the Timeline (it will be highlighted). 3. Tap the Add button on the far left of the Main Toolbar (the same button you used to import your first video). 4. Select the video or photo you want to use as your overlay from your camera roll. 5. Tap Add at the bottom right. Alternatively, you can tap the Overlay icon (which looks like two overlapping squares) directly in the Main Toolbar, then select Add Overlay. Once you do this, you will notice a few things change on your screen: The Timeline: A new, thinner track appears directly above your main video track. Your newly added clip sits here. The Preview Screen: The overlay clip appears in the center of the screen, but it is likely much smaller than your main video. 8. Exporting and Posting to TikTok

You have spent hours perfecting your video. You navigated the New Project setup, mastered Basic Cutting and Trimming to get the pacing just right, and layered in a trending track using your Audio and Sound Design skills. You added engaging Text, Captions, and Stickers, smoothed the cuts with Transitions and Visual Effects, and maybe even used Speed Control and Keyframes or Overlays and Background Removal to create something truly unique. Your masterpiece is sitting on your Timeline, waiting for the world to see it. But if you do not export and upload it correctly, all that hard work can be undone. Export a video with the wrong settings, and TikTok might compress it into a blurry, pixelated mess. Forget to toggle one specific setting, and your video will be stamped with a CapCut watermark that instantly signals "amateur" to your viewers. Exporting is the process of turning your project—along with all its separate clips, layers, and effects—into a single, playable video file. Let’s walk through exactly how to finalize your video, get it off the CapCut Timeline, and push it directly to TikTok in the highest possible quality. Understanding Export Settings Before you hit the export button, you need to tell CapCut exactly how you want your video file to be built. This requires understanding three crucial settings: resolution, frame rate, and the watermark toggle. What is Resolution? Resolution refers to the physical size of your video, measured in pixels. Pixels are the tiny individual dots of light that make up the image on your screen. A video’s resolution is written as a width-by-height measurement. Because TikTok is designed for vertical viewing on smartphones, the standard shape (or aspect ratio) for TikTok videos is 9:16. This means the video is taller than it is wide. The ideal resolution for TikTok is 1080p, which translates to 1080 pixels wide by 1920 pixels tall (often written as 1080 x 1920). If you choose a lower resolution, like 720p, your video will still play on TikTok, but it won’t look as sharp on modern smartphone screens. If you choose a higher resolution, like 4K (2160 x 3840), your file size will be massive. While TikTok does support 4K uploads, the file takes much longer to export and upload, and the average viewer watching on a phone won't notice a massive difference compared to 1080p. For the best balance of quality and upload speed, 1080p is the sweet spot. What is Frame Rate? Frame rate refers to how many individual pictures (frames) are displayed every second to create the illusion of motion. It is measured in frames per second, or fps.
