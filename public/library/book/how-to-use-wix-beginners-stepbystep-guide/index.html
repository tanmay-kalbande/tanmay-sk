1. Getting Started with Wix

A Real‑World Prompt: “I Need a Website by Tomorrow” Imagine Maya, a freelance graphic designer who just landed her first client. The client asks for a simple portfolio site that can be live within 24 hours. Maya has never built a website before, but she’s heard that Wix lets anyone create a professional‑looking site without writing code. She signs in, picks a plan, and in a few clicks, her site is online. If Maya can do it, so can you. This chapter walks you through every step needed to get your Wix account up and running, understand the main parts of the dashboard, and decide whether the Wix ADI (Artificial Design Intelligence) or the Wix Editor is the right tool for your first site. --- 1. Creating Your Wix Account 1.1 Why an Account Matters An account is your passport to every Wix service—hosting, templates, apps, and support. All your sites, settings, and billing information live under a single login, which makes it easy to manage multiple projects from one place. 1.2 Step‑by‑Step Sign‑Up 1. Visit the homepage – Go to www.wix.com using any modern web browser (Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Safari). 2. Click “Get Started” – The button is usually bright orange and located near the top of the page. 3. Enter your email address – Use an email you check regularly; Wix will send a verification link. 4. Create a password – Choose a strong password (at least 8 characters, mixing letters, numbers, and symbols). 5. Confirm your email – Open the verification email from Wix and click the link. You’ll be redirected back to Wix, now logged in. 4. Building Pages and Managing Site Structure

Why a Clear Site Structure Matters Imagine you are opening a virtual storefront for a boutique coffee shop. A visitor lands on the homepage, sees a tempting photo of a latte, and wants to learn more about the shop’s story, the full drink menu, and how to place an online order. If the site’s pages are scattered, the menu is tangled, and links are missing, the visitor will quickly become frustrated and leave. A well‑organized page hierarchy and intuitive navigation do three things for beginners like you: Guide visitors to the information they need in as few clicks as possible. Signal professionalism to both users and search engines. Make future updates (adding a new “Seasonal Specials” page, for example) painless. The steps below walk you through creating, arranging, and linking pages in Wix so that every visitor can find their way—no matter how many pages you eventually add. --- Adding New Pages 1. Open the Wix Editor From the Wix Dashboard → My Sites, click Select Site and then Edit Site. The editor you explored in Understanding the Wix Editor Interface will appear. 2. Locate the Pages Panel On the left‑hand side, click the Pages icon (it looks like a stack of sheets). This panel lists every page currently in your site. 3. Create a Blank Page 1. Click + Add Page at the bottom of the panel. 2. In the dialog, type a descriptive name (e.g., Menu, About Us, Contact). 3. Choose Blank Page if you want to start from scratch, or pick a Pre‑Designed Layout (covered next). 4. Click Done. The new page appears in the panel and opens on the canvas. Tip: Keep page names short and intuitive; these names become the default menu labels unless you edit them later. 4. Duplicate an Existing Page (Optional) If you need a page with a similar layout (say, multiple “Event” pages), hover over an existing page in the panel, click the three‑dot menu, and select Duplicate. Rename the copy to fit its purpose. --- Choosing a Page Layout Wix offers page layouts—pre‑arranged structures of sections, columns, and placeholders—that speed up design. 1. Access Layout Options When you add a new page, the Add Page dialog includes a Layouts tab. Click it to browse categories such as Business, Portfolio, Blog, and Online Store. 2. Tip: If you need a pre‑styled block (e.g., a paragraph with a background color), choose one of the Text Presets that appear under the same menu. Using Headings for Structure Headings (H1, H2, H3, …) are the hierarchy that tells both readers and search engines how your content is organized. - H1 – Use only once per page, typically for the main title (“Our Menu”). - H2 – Sub‑sections (“Cakes”, “Pastries”). - H3 – Further detail (“Chocolate Cake”, “Gluten‑Free Muffins”). To change a text box into a heading: 1. Click the box to open the Text Toolbar. 2. Click the Paragraph dropdown and select Heading 2 (or whichever level you need). 6. Design Basics: Themes, Colors, and Fonts

Choosing a Preset Theme Imagine you’ve just created a Wix site for a boutique coffee shop. The owner loves the vibe of a modern, minimalist coffee house but the site still looks like a blank canvas. The fastest way to give the site personality is to apply a preset theme – a collection of colors, fonts, and layout styles that Wix bundles together. 1. Open the Wix Editor (you already did this in Understanding the Wix Editor Interface). 2. Click the “Design” button on the left‑hand toolbar. 3. Select “Theme Manager.” Here you’ll see a gallery of ready‑made themes such as Elegant, Bold, Playful, and Minimalist. When you hover over a theme, Wix shows a live preview of how your current page would look with that theme applied. Pick the one that best matches the coffee shop’s brand personality – for our scenario, Minimalist works well. Tip: Choosing a theme does not lock you into every element it contains. You can later tweak colors, fonts, and spacing while keeping the overall look consistent. Once you click “Apply”, the theme instantly updates all pages you’ve built so far, giving you a cohesive visual foundation to work from. --- Modifying the Theme Palette A palette is the set of colors the theme uses for backgrounds, text, buttons, and accents. Changing the palette lets you align the site with the brand’s specific colors (e.g., the coffee shop’s deep brown and creamy ivory). Where to Find the Palette 1. In the Theme Manager, click “Colors.” 2. You’ll see a row of color swatches labeled Primary, Secondary, Accent, and Background. Editing a Swatch - Click any swatch. A color picker appears with three ways to choose a new hue: Palette grid – a set of popular colors. Hex code field – type a precise web color code (e.g., 4B2E2A). RGB sliders – adjust red, green, and blue values manually. Jargon note: A hex code is a six‑character code that represents a specific color on the web. It always starts with a . Practical Example The coffee shop’s logo uses 4B2E2A for the beans and F5EEDC for the cream background. - Change the Primary swatch to 4B2E2A. - Change the Background swatch to F5EEDC. All elements that previously used the generic “Primary” color (like button backgrounds and heading highlights) now adopt the new deep‑brown tone, instantly tying the site to the brand. Saving and Naming Your Palette After adjusting the colors, click “Save As New Palette.” Give it a descriptive name like CoffeeShop‑Brand. 7. Mobile Optimization

A Phone‑First Reality Check Imagine you’ve just launched a fresh Wix site for your boutique coffee shop. You share the link with a friend, and she opens it on her iPhone while waiting in line. The beautiful hero image you set on the desktop stretches off‑screen, the navigation menu is hidden behind a tiny icon, and the “Call Now” button she needs to place an order doesn’t work at all. She’s impressed by the design on a laptop, but on her phone the experience feels clunky—and she quickly taps away. That moment is a common wake‑up call for beginners: a site that looks perfect on a desktop can fall apart on a mobile device. Since more than half of global web traffic now arrives from smartphones and tablets, mobile optimization isn’t a nice‑to‑have; it’s essential. In Wix, you can fine‑tune the mobile version of your site without starting from scratch. Let’s walk through the process step by step. --- Switching to Mobile View 1. Open the Wix Editor If you’re still on the Wix Editor after completing the earlier modules (Choosing a Template, Adding Content, etc.), you’re already where you need to be. 1. Log in at www.wix.com and select My Sites. 2. Click Edit Site for the project you want to work on. 2. Locate the Mobile Icon At the top toolbar of the editor, you’ll see two small icons side by side: - Desktop View (the default) - Mobile View (a phone silhouette) Tip: The icons look like a computer monitor and a smartphone. Hovering over them displays a tooltip that reads “Switch to Mobile”. Click the Mobile View icon. Wix instantly reloads the editor, showing a preview of how your site will appear on a typical smartphone screen (around 375 px wide). Note: You are now in a mobile‑only editing mode. Any changes you make here affect only the mobile version; the desktop layout remains unchanged. 3. Understand the Mobile Editing Panel When in Mobile View, a new panel slides in from the right side labeled Mobile Settings. Here you can: - Turn Hide on Mobile on/off for any element - Adjust Mobile Menu options - Enable Tap‑to‑Call for phone numbers Familiarize yourself with this panel—most of the mobile‑specific controls live here. --- How Mobile View Differs From Desktop View 1. Stacked Layout On a desktop, elements can sit side‑by‑side because there’s plenty of horizontal space. Mobile screens are narrow, so Wix automatically stacks columns and strips vertically. 8. Basic SEO Settings for Wix Sites

The design looks great, the menu is easy to read, and the site works perfectly on mobile—thanks to the work you did in earlier chapters. Yet, when a potential customer searches for “cupcake shop near me,” the bakery doesn’t appear in the results. Why? Because search engines need clear signals about what each page is about, how it should be indexed, and how it relates to user queries. By tweaking a handful of basic SEO settings—page titles, meta descriptions, URL slugs, image alt text, and schema markup—you can turn Sweet Treats from invisible to discoverable. This chapter walks you through those essential tweaks inside Wix, step by step. --- 1. What Is “Basic SEO” and Why Does It Matter? SEO (Search Engine Optimization) is the practice of helping search engines understand and rank your website. Think of a search engine as a librarian; the clearer your book’s label and summary, the easier the librarian can place it on the right shelf. Four core signals we’ll focus on: | Signal | What It Is | Why It Helps | |--------|------------|--------------| | Page Title | The clickable headline shown in search results. | Directly influences click‑through rates and contains the primary keyword. | | Meta Description | A short paragraph that appears under the title in search results. | Summarizes the page’s content; good descriptions improve click‑through rates. | | URL Slug | The part of the web address after the domain (e.g., /cupcake-menu). | Clean, keyword‑rich URLs are easier for both users and search engines to read. | | Alt Text | Textual description attached to an image. | Provides context for visually impaired users and gives search engines clues about the image. | | Schema Markup | Structured data that tells search engines about specific content types (e.g., recipes, local business). | Enables rich results like star ratings, price ranges, or opening hours. | Even if you’ve never heard these terms before, the steps below will guide you through each one using the Wix Editor and Wix SEO Wiz. --- 2. 10. Publishing, Domain Management, and Ongoing Maintenance

Publishing Your Wix Site Imagine you’ve spent the last week designing Sweet Crumbs, a cozy bakery website, using the Wix Editor. The colors match the brand, the menu page lists all pastries, and the contact form is ready to take orders. The moment of truth has arrived: you need to push the site from the editor sandbox to the world‑wide web. 1. Preview – the final safety net Before you click Publish, use the built‑in preview tools you explored in Understanding the Wix Editor Interface. 1. Desktop preview – Click the eye‑icon at the top‑right of the editor. Scroll through each page, test every button, and make sure the navigation works as expected. 2. Mobile preview – Switch to the phone icon. Wix automatically creates a mobile‑friendly version, but you can still tweak element sizes or hide items that don’t translate well. 3. External preview link – In the preview toolbar, select “View Site”. This opens a temporary URL (e.g., username.wixsite.com/sweetcrumbs) in a new tab, letting you experience the site exactly as visitors will. Tip: Open the preview link on a different device or ask a friend to test it. Fresh eyes often spot broken links or confusing copy that you’ve grown blind to. 2. Publishing – turning the lights on When you’re satisfied with the preview, it’s time to publish. 1. Locate the Publish button – It sits in the upper‑right corner of the Wix Editor, next to the preview eye. 2. Click Publish – Wix will process your changes and generate a live version. A small toast notification appears: “Your site is now live at username.wixsite.com/sweetcrumbs.” 3. Verify the live URL – Click the “Visit Site” link in the notification or copy the URL into a fresh browser tab. Confirm that: All pages load without error. Images appear in high resolution (no blurry placeholders). Forms submit successfully (you’ll receive a test email). If any issue pops up, return to the editor, fix it, and Publish again. Wix updates the live site instantly—no waiting for a “build queue.” 3. By tapping the Wix Analytics dashboard and linking a Google Analytics (GA) property, Maya can pinpoint exactly where visitors drop off, test small changes, and watch the conversion rate climb. The steps below walk you through that exact process, from opening the dashboard to making data‑driven tweaks that keep your site improving over time. --- Understanding the Wix Analytics Dashboard Getting to the Dashboard 1.
